## 1. Scientific Principles of Spatial Crash Analysis

When presenting road accident maps to transport planners, municipal engineers, or the public, three fundamentally distinct quantities must never be conflated:

1. **Observed Accident Count:** The raw tally of recorded incidents within a geographic boundary.
2. **Crash Density:** The number of recorded incidents per unit surface area ($\text{accidents}/\text{km}^2$).
3. **True Exposure-Adjusted Crash Risk:** The probability of a crash per million vehicle-kilometers traveled (VKT) or per thousand pedestrians crossing.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Because comprehensive vehicle volume and pedestrian counts are unavailable in the source data, all maps and hotspot tables generated by AccidentVision AI depict **observed crash concentration and density**, NOT intrinsic structural hazard. A busy six-lane arterial may accumulate 50 crashes per year with a low individual risk per trip, whereas a poorly lit rural curve may register 10 crashes with an extraordinarily high risk per passage.

## 4. OpenStreetMap Highway Enrichment Coverage

- **Total OSM-Enriched Records:** 15,425 (77.1%)
- **Primary Road Classes Identified:** `trunk`, `primary`, `secondary`, `tertiary`, `residential`, `motorway`
- **Mean Road Length:** 1,842.6 meters
- **Mean Distance to Matched Road Centerline:** 18.4 meters
- **High/Exact Match Quality:** 15,425 records
- **Unmatched Mumbai Records:** 642 records in Mumbai lacked road distance vectors due to the spatial bounding extent of the local OSM extract.

## 5. Hotspot Identification via DBSCAN

By applying DBSCAN with Haversine metric ($\varepsilon = 500\text{m}$, $\text{min\_samples} = 15$ crashes):
- Hotspots cluster around high-density multi-lane junctions and arterial junctions.
- In Delhi, high-density clusters are found along major radial corridors (Ring Road / Outer Ring Road connections).
- In Bangalore, prominent clusters emerge along Outer Ring Road (ORR) and Hosur Road corridors.
- In Mumbai, concentrations align with the Western Express Highway (WEH) and Eastern Express Highway (EEH) arteries.

Interactive visualization is provided in the AccidentVision AI dashboard with dynamic filtering by city, severity, road class, and time window.
